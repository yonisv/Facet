# Facet Marketing Campaign

Marketing strategy and creative brief for Facet, a modular fitness tracker concept. One sensor pod docks into a sport shell, a watch shell, or a bracelet shell. Facet sells as a one-time purchase with no required subscription. This repository holds the campaign work: the creative brief, the marketing strategy, and the behavioral model behind them.

## The product idea

Most trackers are built around one setting, usually the gym. Facet is built for people whose days cross several settings, such as a practice, a classroom, an office, and a dinner. The pod stays the same and only the shell changes. Basic recovery, sleep, and activity tracking come included. Advanced metrics are optional packages.

## Campaign stages

| Stage | Goal |
|---|---|
| 1. Awareness | Make Facet memorable and set it alongside Apple, Garmin, and WHOOP. |
| 2. Problem recognition | Turn the frustration of a watch that feels wrong in the wrong setting into a problem Facet solves. |
| 3. Search | Answer questions on price, accuracy, battery life, privacy, and shell switching. |
| 4. Alternative evaluation | Position Facet as the lower-risk choice against Apple Watch, Garmin, WHOOP, Fitbit, and Oura. |
| 5. Choice | Guide first-time buyers with a "Find Your Facet" quiz instead of a product grid. |
| 6. Purchase and onboarding | Confirm total cost before payment, teach shell switching early, and deliver quick early wins. |
| 7. Post-purchase outcomes | Keep owners wearing the device as their routines change, and recover well when something goes wrong. |

## Key strategy points

- Versatility is the central message: one tracker for each part of the day.
- Subscriptions are never required. The strategy deck notes that WHOOP and Garmin lock core features behind $20 to $30 per month on top of the device price.
- The tone shifts by stage. It starts empathetic, becomes precise during search, turns reassuring during onboarding, and stays accountable after purchase. The campaign makes no exaggerated health claims.

## My part

I owned the Purchase and Onboarding and Post-Purchase Outcomes stages (6 and 7). Those cover order and shipping messages, the quick-start card in the box, welcome emails on days 1, 3, and 7, in-app milestone celebrations, and shell-switching guidance. They also cover retention work: trend summaries, referral prompts, and service recovery.

## Open questions

The strategy deck leaves four questions open:

- Are multiple shells useful for workouts, school, and work, or too complicated?
- Should the data show simple lifestyle metrics, complex performance metrics, or a mix?
- Is a higher up-front price with add-ons better than a lower price with a monthly subscription?
- What gives a customer a reason to leave an established brand for Facet?
